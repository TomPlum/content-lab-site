Privacy policy

Last updated 28 September 2026

Content Lab is a private application that Tom Plumpton (“the owner”) runs on his own computer to publish and review original short videos on his own Instagram, TikTok and YouTube accounts. This policy explains what the application accesses on each platform, how it uses and stores that information, who else receives it, and how to remove it. It also covers this information website, separately, at the end.

Content Lab uses the YouTube API Services. By using it, you agree to be bound by the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's handling of your data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.

Who uses Content Lab

Only the owner signs in to Content Lab or connects accounts to it, and only accounts he owns: Loop Mechanics on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, and The Third Door on Instagram. The application runs on the owner's computer and is not available to anyone else. It does not access other people's accounts.

Content Lab does not receive information about individual viewers or followers. The performance figures it reads are counts and averages for the owner's own accounts and posts, and it does not read the text of comments.

How Content Lab connects

  1. Instagram, through the owner's own Meta app.
  2. The Google sign-in, which reads the YouTube channel's identity and analytics.
  3. Zernio, a third-party publishing service, which publishes approved videos to TikTok and YouTube.

1. Instagram

Content Lab uses the Instagram API with Instagram Login, through a Meta app the owner created for this purpose. Each Instagram account has a long-lived access token that the owner generates in that app.

Information accessed

  • Account identity: the account's Instagram user ID and username, checked before each publication so a video is not posted to the wrong account.
  • The account's own posts: each post's ID, caption, link and publication time, used to confirm a publication and to avoid posting the same video twice.
  • Publishing status: whether an uploaded video has finished processing, and how much of Instagram's daily publishing limit has been used.
  • Post insights: for each published post, views, reach, likes, number of comments, shares, saves and average watch time, collected when the owner runs an import, usually about 24 hours, 3 days and 7 days after publication.

Information sent

To publish a Reel, Content Lab sends Instagram the caption and a temporary web address for the video. Instagram only accepts video this way, so Content Lab makes that one file reachable through a temporary Cloudflare tunnel at a random address, and closes the tunnel as soon as Instagram reports that it has processed the video, usually within a minute. Nothing else on the owner's computer is exposed.

2. The Google sign-in

Permissions requested

View your YouTube account (youtube.readonly)
Used to identify the connected channel and, when the owner asks, to read details of the channel's own videos, such as the title, description and privacy status, to confirm that a publication appeared as approved.
View YouTube Analytics reports for your YouTube content (yt-analytics.readonly)
Used to read channel analytics when the owner asks Content Lab to sync them.

Content Lab does not request permission to upload or manage videos: it does not upload through the Google sign-in, and publication goes through Zernio, described below. It does not request access to revenue reports, Gmail, Google Drive, contacts or any other Google service.

Information accessed and stored

  • Sign-in credentials: the access token and refresh token Google issues, when the access token expires, and the permissions granted.
  • Channel identity: the channel ID, channel title and handle, when the connection was last checked, and whether the owner confirmed it is the right channel.
  • Channel analytics: daily channel totals for the 28 days ending the previous day, covering views, engaged views, estimated minutes watched, average view duration, likes, comments, shares and subscribers gained, together with the date range requested and when it was collected.
  • Sign-in in progress: while the owner is signing in, a random state value and a verification code used to complete the sign-in securely. They expire after ten minutes and are deleted once used.

This information is used only to show the owner which channel is connected, to check a publication, and to show how the channel is performing in the owner's local dashboard. Analytics are fetched only when the owner requests a sync. None of it is sent to Zernio, Meta or TikTok.

Limited Use

Content Lab's use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

3. Zernio, for TikTok and YouTube publishing

The owner's TikTok account and YouTube channel are connected to Zernio within the owner's Zernio account. Those connections are authorised by the owner on Zernio's own terms, not through Content Lab. Content Lab uses Zernio's API, with the owner's Zernio key, to publish videos the owner has approved.

Information sent

  • The video file. Zernio stores uploaded media at a web address that is publicly reachable to anyone who has it; Zernio describes it as temporary for seven days and copied to permanent storage when published.
  • For TikTok: the caption, and the post settings (who can view it, whether comments, duets and stitches are allowed, and the commercial-content and AI-generated disclosures).
  • For YouTube: the title and description, including hashtags and any music credit, and the settings for visibility, whether it is made for children, whether it contains altered or synthetic content, and its category.

Information received

  • The connected account's identity, checked before each publication.
  • For TikTok, the posting options TikTok allows the account, such as the permitted visibility settings and maximum video length.
  • Each publication's status, identifiers and link, and the captions and times of the account's recent posts known to Zernio, used to avoid posting the same video twice and to respect a daily limit.

Content Lab keeps a local record of each publication: which video it was, a checksum of the file, when it was attempted, its status, and the identifiers and links Zernio returns. Zernio's handling of this information is described in Zernio's privacy policy.

How information is used

Content Lab uses the information above only to publish the owner's approved videos to the right accounts, to avoid duplicate or mistaken publications, and to show the owner how his accounts and posts are performing. It does not use it for advertising, sell it, or use it to train artificial intelligence models.

Where information is stored

Everything above is stored in files and local databases on the owner's computer, in private folders and configuration files that are excluded from version control. Files holding the Google sign-in and YouTube analytics can be read only by the owner's user account on that computer. Content Lab does not encrypt this information itself, and there is no Content Lab server or cloud database.

Who else receives information

  • Meta, TikTok, Google and YouTube receive the requests Content Lab makes to their services, and the videos and text published to them.
  • Zernio receives approved videos and their text, as described above.
  • Cloudflare carries each Instagram video through the temporary tunnel while Instagram fetches it.
  • AI assistants. The owner operates Content Lab with the help of AI coding assistants (Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's Codex) running on his computer. When the owner asks one to carry out a Content Lab task, such as publishing a video, syncing analytics or checking a publication, what it reads while doing so, which can include account identity and performance figures, is sent to that assistant's provider to process the request, under that provider's terms.

Content Lab does not sell information, share it with advertisers, or allow third parties to serve content or advertisements through it.

Cookies and device storage

While the owner signs in with Google, Content Lab places a single cookie in the owner's browser to tie the sign-in to that browser. It cannot be read by scripts, lasts at most ten minutes and is cleared when the sign-in finishes. Content Lab uses no advertising or analytics cookies. The information described above is stored on the owner's computer as explained under “Where information is stored”.

How long information is kept

  • Access tokens and keys are kept until the owner removes or replaces them, or until the platform stops accepting them. Instagram tokens last about 60 days unless refreshed.
  • YouTube channel analytics: each sync replaces the previous report. The latest report is kept until the owner deletes it.
  • Instagram insights and the workspace: kept until the owner deletes them. The workspace also keeps earlier versions of itself, so removing something from the current workspace does not remove it from that history.
  • Publishing records are kept until the owner deletes them. They are what stop the same video being published twice.

Revoking access and deleting information

You can revoke Content Lab's access to Google data at any time via the Google security settings page at https://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions. The data deletion page explains how to revoke access on each platform and how to delete the information Content Lab keeps.

This information website

This website, content.tomplumpton.me, is separate from the application. It has no sign-in, forms, cookies, analytics or scripts of its own, and loads no third-party fonts or embedded content. It is hosted by GitHub Pages, and its domain name is served by Cloudflare's DNS. GitHub processes information about visits, such as IP addresses, to deliver and protect the site, as described in the GitHub General Privacy Statement. The owner does not receive visitor logs from GitHub.

Changes
