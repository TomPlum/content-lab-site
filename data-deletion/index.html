Data deletion

Last updated 6 October 2026

Content Lab only connects to accounts belonging to its owner, and keeps what it collects on the owner's computer. This page explains how to stop each connection and how to delete what Content Lab keeps. The privacy policy describes that information in full.

If you think Content Lab holds information about you, or you want information deleted, email tomplum.content@gmail.com. The owner will reply, tell you what Content Lab holds, and confirm once it has been deleted.

1. Revoke access

Instagram

  • Remove the app under Apps and websites in the Instagram account's settings (instagram.com/accounts/manage_access).
  • Delete the account's access token from Content Lab's local configuration. Without it, Content Lab cannot reach the account.

Facebook

  • Remove the app under Business integrations in the Facebook account's settings (facebook.com/settings?tab=business_tools). This invalidates the Page token.
  • Delete the Page token from Content Lab's local configuration.

Google and YouTube

  • Revoke Content Lab's access via the Google security settings page at https://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions.
  • Disconnect on Content Lab's local YouTube page. This deletes the saved sign-in credentials, channel identity and any sign-in in progress, but keeps analytics and publishing records, and does not revoke access at Google by itself.

TikTok and YouTube publishing through Zernio

  • Disconnect the account in Zernio, or remove Zernio's access in the TikTok or Google account's settings for connected apps.
  • Delete the Zernio key from Content Lab's local configuration.

2. Delete what Content Lab keeps

  • YouTube analytics: delete the analytics file in Content Lab's private YouTube data folder.
  • Instagram and Facebook insights, and Instagram publishing details: these are held in Content Lab's local workspace database. Delete them from the workspace and from its history of earlier versions, or delete the database.
  • Facebook, TikTok and YouTube publishing records: held in local databases, one per platform. Delete them only after checking that no publication is still in progress or unresolved. Content Lab uses these records to confirm whether an attempt reached the platform, and deleting an unresolved one could lead to the same video being published twice.

3. What Content Lab cannot delete for you

Published videos, and the copies and records held by Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube and Zernio, are kept by those services under their own policies. Delete videos in the Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or YouTube apps, delete posts and media in Zernio, and use each service's own tools to delete account data.
